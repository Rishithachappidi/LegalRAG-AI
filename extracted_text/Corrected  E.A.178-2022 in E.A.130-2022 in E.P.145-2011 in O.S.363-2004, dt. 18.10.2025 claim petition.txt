In reply to the above, the learned counsel for respondents 2 to

A.P. and others, reported in (2007) 4 SCC 221.
12
27. 
In view of the rival contentions of both parties, the question 
before the court is whether the decree and judgment in O.S. 947/2008 is a 
collusive one, and if so, whether it is necessary for respondents 2 to 4 to file 
a separate suit to nullify the judgment and decree in O.S. 947/2008, or can 
they challenge the decree and judgment in collateral proceedings like the 
present claim petition.
Can a judgment and decree in another suit be challenged in the collateral 
proceedings?
28.
It is true, a judgment, decree or decree obtained by playing 
fraud on the court, tribunal or authority is a nullity and non est in the eye of 
the law2. Further, fraud avoids all judicial acts, ecclesiastical or temporal3. In 
other words, fraud and collusion vitiate even the most solemn proceedings 
in any civilized system of jurisprudence4. Such a judgment, decree or order 
by the first court or by the final court has to be treated as a nullity by every 
court, superior or inferior. It can be challenged in any court, at any time, in 
appeal, revision, writ or even in collateral proceedings5.
29.
Admittedly, respondents 2 to 4 are not parties to the suit in 
O.S.947/2008 and its subsequent proceedings. But according to them, it is 
a collusive judgment and decree, and it is non est in the eye of law. No 
doubt, they can challenge the judgment and decree by filing a separate suit 
or challenge the same in collateral proceedings like the present claim 
petition as held in the above authorities.

defendant is trying to execute agreement and document in

of the plaintiff and there is no iota of truth in that averment.”
38. 
This written statement appears to be prepared in the year 2004. 
The above pleadings create doubt on the existence of possessory sale 
agreement, connecting to O.S.947/2008, by the date of filing of the written 
statement. So, as per the above defence of the 5th respondent herein who is 
defendant in OS 363 of 2004, there is no possessory sale agreement by the 
date of filing written statement. Admittedly, the 5th respondent contested the 
main suit (OS 363 of 2004) and after disposal of suit, he filed an appeal. If 
there really was a possessory sale agreement connecting to OS 947 of 
2008 prior to the suit in OS 363 of 2004 or the suit sale agreement therein, 
the 5th respondent would have stated the same in his pleading or at least 
inform existence of possessory sale agrement in the trial court during trial or 
appeal proceedings. But he never revealed about possessory sale 
16
agreement connecting to the suit in OS 947 of 2008 at any stage of the 
proceedings of suit in OS 363 of 2004 or its connected appeal. This conduct 
of the fifth respondent does not probabilize the claim of petitioner herein 
regarding the existence of possessory sale agreement by the date of suit in 
OS 363 of 2004. Further, his conduct creates a strong doubt on the 
genuineness of the claim of Narasimha Swamy in the suit in O.S.947/2008.
39. 
In so far as the third aspect is concerned, learned counsel for 
respondents 2 to 4 has drawn the attention of this court to the possessory 
sale agreement, judgment and decree, E.P. and E.A. proceedings 
connecting to the suit in O.S.947/2008 and submitted that there is a

40. 
In view of arguments of learned counsel, the possessory sale

Possessory sale agreement/Ex.P5:
41.
Possessory sale agreement/Ex.P5 appears that it was

43.

47. 
Generally, when the vendee is not delivered possession under

Let me examine the delivery receipt/Ex.P3. It reveals that as

19

It is significant to note that the suit in O.S.947/2008 was filed at

were completed in O.S.947/2008. But the 5th respondent never revealed the

respondent and Narasimha Swamy in filing suit and obtaining an exparte

50.
Thus, it is established from the above circumstances that the 
suit in O.S.947/2008 was filed at the fag end of suit in O.S.363/2004, based 
on a possessory sale agreement alleged to have been executed in the year 
2002. Further, it is established that though the fifth respondent contested 
the suit in O.S.363/2004 and filed appeal, he allowed to pass the decree of 
the suit in O.S.947/2008 without contesting or defending the suit and 
subsequent proceedings as well. Further, he never informed in the suit 
(O.S.363/2004) or its connected appeal proceedings about the alleged 
possessory sale agreement of suit in O.S.947/2009 and delivery of property. 
Further, it is established from the above that the fifth respondent protracted 
the suit in O.S.363/2004 and also filed an appeal and dragon the litigation 
till the completion of the suit, execution and delivery proceedings in 
O.S.947/2008. It is further established from the above that though 
possession was delivered in E.A.88/2010 connecting to the suit in 
O.S.947/2008, he never informed in appeal proceedings. Further, it is 
established that though possession was not delivered to Narasimha Swamy 
(Dhr therein) under the sale agreement, he filed a suit and obtained 
judgment and a sale deed as if possession were delivered to him.
20
51. 
All the above circumstances establish that to defeat the claim of 
the 1st respondent herein, who is the plaintiff in the suit in O.S.363/2004, the 
suit (O.S.947/2008) is filed, obtained exparte decree, sale deed and

Non-examination of the fifth respondent and Narasimha Swamy (Dhr 
in O.S.947/2008):
52. 
Petitioner contends that the suit and subsequent proceedings in

53. 
To run off the situation, learned counsel for petitioner would

Narasimha Swamy (Dhr therein). Though he does not know them, he can 
summon them to prove the proceedings in O.S. 947/2008 are not collusive 
in nature. But he did not do it. Therefore, the petitioner cannot escape from 
examining them on the ground that he does not know them. Thus, the 
petitioner failed to disprove that the above circumstances are not collusive 
proceedings in O.S.947/2008.
Whether the sale deed obtained by Narasimha Swamy prevails over 
the sale deed of first respondent, as it is a prior document:
54. 
To wriggle out of the situation, learned counsel for petitioner 
would submit that the sale deed of Narasimha Swamy, which is the source 
for the flow of title to the petitioner, is the first document and prevails over 
the sale deed of the first respondent. Further, learned counsel submitted 
that once the sale deed is executed by the court on behalf of fifth 
respondent in favour of Narasimha Swamy, the fifth respondent has no right 
to sell away the same property later, and therefore, no right or title would be 
conferred on the first respondent under the subsequent registered sale 
21
deed.
55.
Learned counsel rightly submitted that as per Sec.48 of the 
Transfer of Property Act and Sec.47 of the Indian Registration Act, a 
registered sale deed which is registered first would prevail over a later 
document. Once a person alienates his property to another, later he cannot 
alienate it again to another. This principle is based on latin Maxim of nemo 
dat quod non habet. It means no one can pass a better title than what he 
has.
56.
In the present case, III AJCJC, Guntur, executed a sale deed 
on behalf of the fifth respondent herein in favour of Narasimha Swamy on 
25.11.2009. Thereafter, V ASCJC, Guntur, executed a sale deed in 
E.P.145/2011 in favour of the first respondent herein. Thus, the sale deed of 
Narasimha Swamy is first, and sale deed obtained the first respondent is 
later document. But it is stated above that the proceedings in the suit in 
O.S.947/2008 are collusive in nature and Narasimha Swamy obtained a 
collusive decree. As judgment is obtained collusively, a registered sale deed 
executed based on a collusive decree will not convey any right or title, 
because fraud vitiated all rights, including solemn acts, as stated above. 
Therefore, though the Narasimha Swamy obtained a registered sale deed 
prior to the sale deed of first respondent, it will not confer any right or title on 
Narasimha Swamy, as judgment obtained collusively. Therefore, the above 
provisions and Latin maxim are not applicable to the present case. Hence, 
the contentions of learned counsel are not tenable.
Bonafide purchaser:
57. 
In order to get over the situation, learned counsel for petitioner 
would submit that the petitioner is a bonafide purchaser for valid 
consideration without knowledge of the suit or other proceedings, and he 
came to know for the first time about the suit proceedings when the field

22

58.
On the other hand, learned counsel for respondents 2 to 4 
would submit that the petitioner is not a bonafide purchaser, and it is

while purchasing the property, the purchaser would verify documents and 
the encumbrance certificate to know whether there are any other alienations 
over the property. The sale deed of first respondent is the prior sale deed of 
petitioner’s father, and it would be reflected in the encumbrance certificate, 
but in spite of said fact, he purchased the property, and therefore, there are 
no bonafidies on the petitioner, further learned counsel submitted.
59. 
Indisputably, the plea of bonafide purchaser is one of the 
grounds to defend the case. Petitioner claims that his father is a bonafide 
purchaser for valuable consideration without having knowledge about 
proceedings in the present E.A. and connected suit in O.S.363/2004. The 
father of the petitioner obtained a registered sale deed on 30.3.2013. His 
vendor (PW2) obtained the sale deed on 14.8.2012. V Addl. Senior Civil 
Judge, Guntur, executed sale deed in favour first respondent on 12.7.2012. 
It shows that prior to the sale deed obtained by the vendor of petitioner’s 
father (PW2), V ASCJC, Guntur, executed a registered sale deed. This fact 
will be reflected in the encumbrance certificate.
60.
It is relevant to mention here that PW2 admitted in his cross-
examination that before purchasing the property, he verified the 
Encumbrance Certificate; and that the father of petitioner also obtained the 
Encumbrance Certificate. It is beneficial to reproduce the relevant portion of 
evidence of PW2 hereunder:
“Before my purchase, I got verified encumbrance 
certificate. It is recited in my sale deed/Ex.P9 that 
encumbrance certificate  was handed over.”
“I sold schedule property to Gude Subbaiah, I handed 
over all the documents relating to schedule property to 
23
Subbaiah. At the time of purchase, Gude Subbaiah also 
obtained encumbrance certificate.”
61. 
Thus, it is established from the above that before purchasing 
property, PW2 and the petitioner’s father verified the encumbrance 
certificate. If that being so, the sale deed obtained by the first respondent 
would be reflected in the encumbrance certificate. Consequently, the 
litigation on the schedule property would be known. Therefore, now they 
cannot contend that they have no knowledge about the suit proceedings 
and sale deed of the first respondent. Hence, there are no bonafidies as 
contended by the learned counsel for the petitioner.
62. 
Whatever the above, it is already stated above that Narasimha 
Swamy obtained a decree in O.S.947/2008 collusively, and no title will be 
passed to Narasimha Swamy under the registered sale deed. 
Consequently, title would not confer on the sale deed obtained by PW2 
(father of petitioner) and his vendor’s sale deed. Therefore, the plea of 
bonafide purchaser cannot defeat the judgment and decree in O.S.No. 
363/2004 and the sale deed obtained by the first respondent.
Maintainability of E.A.130/2022 on the ground of limitation:
63.
Eventually, learned counsel for petitioner would submit that 
though the decree was passed on 17.08.2009 and the sale deed was 
obtained on 12.7.2012, the execution application for possession is not filed 
within one year as per Art.134 of the Limitation Act6 or 3 years as per 
Art.137 of the Act, and therefore, the execution application in E.A.130/2022 
is barred by limitation. To buttress his contentions, he relied upon judgment 
in Pattam Khader Khan Vs. Rattam Sardar Khan and another, reported in 
(1996) 5 AD (SC) 381. 
64. 
On the other hand, learned counsel for respondents 2 to 4 
would submit that the limitation for filing an application for delivery of 
6
For brevity, “the Act”
24
property in suit for specific performance is 12 years from the date of 
execution of sale deed, but not either one year or three years as argued by 
the learned counsel for petitioner. To fortify his contentions, he relied upon 
judgments in Deep Chand and others Vs. Mohan Lal, reported in (2000) 6 
Supreme Court Cases 259, and G. Subramanyam (died) and others Vs. B. 
Raghunath (died)  and others, reported in (2017) 4 ALT 258.
65. 
In view of the rival contentions of both parties, it is to be 
examined what the limitation is for filing an execution application for delivery 
of property in a suit for specific performance of a sale agreement, and 
whether the application in E.A.130/2022 is filed within the period of 
limitation.
66. 
Coming to Art.134 of the Act, it provides a period of limitation of 
one year for taking possession of property from the date of confirmation of 
sale. In the present case, the property is not sold in court auction. It is a 
decree of specific performance of the sale agreement. Therefore, Art.134 of 
the Act is not applicable to E.A.130/2022.
67. 
Coming to Art.137 of the Schedule to the Act, it is a residuary 
article providing a limitation of three years. This article is relevant when 
limitation is not provided in any of the schedule. Art. 136 provides 12 years 
of limitation when a decree or order becomes enforceable.
68. 
In Deepchand and others case stated supra, our Hon’ble Apex 
Court held that the limitation period of 12 years will start from the date of 
execution of the sale deed in favour of the decree holder.
69. 
Case facts in the above authority are that a sale deed was 
executed in favour of the decree holder as per orders of the court dated 
23.3.1984. Subsequently, in the year 1994, an execution application is filed 
for possession of schedule land. The Hon’ble Supreme Court upheld the 
orders of the Hon’ble High Court that the decree for possession of the land 
becomes enforceable only after execution of the sale deed as per the terms 
25
of the decree. Further, it is held that before the execution of sale deed in 
favour of decree holder on 23.3.1984, the decree holder would not be 
entitled to possession in terms of decree. Therefore, Art. 136 of the Act is 
applicable for filing an execution application for delivery of property in a suit 
for specific performance of a sale agreement.
70. 
Similarly, G. Subrahmanyam’s case stated supra, our Hon’ble 
High Court held that:
“Unless and until the sale deed is executed, either 
by the defendant or on his failure by the court, the plaintiff will 
not become the owner of the suit schedule property. The 
plaintiff, in a suit for specific performance, is not entitled to file 
E.P. for delivery of the schedule property without obtaining sale 
deed. It is needless to say that the decree passed in favour of 
the plaintiff, in a suit for specific performance, will be 
enforceable as contemplated under column No.3 of Article 136 
of the Limitation Act, from the date of obtaining of the 
registered sale deed by the plaintiff-decree holder.”
71. 
It manifests from the above jurimetrical jurisprudence and 
above provisions that the limitation for filing an execution application for 
delivery of property is 12 years from the date of execution of sale deed in 
terms of the decree. Therefore, Art.134 and 137 are not applicable to the 
present case.
72. 
Let me examine whether E.A.130/2022 is filed within limitation. 
V ASCJC, Guntur, executed the sale deed on 12.7.2012. The execution 
application is filed on 11.7.2022. It shows that E.A. for delivery of schedule 
property is filed within the 12 years period of limitation. Thus, it is 
established that the application is filed well within the limitation. Hence, 
contentions of learned counsel are not tenable.
73. 
In the course of arguments, learned counsel for petitioner 
would submit that respondents 2 to 4 have not placed any material why did 
they wait for 10 years, even after obtaining the sale deed, without taking 
26
possession, and it is a probable circumstance to disbelieve the case of 
respondents 2 to 4.
74.
As seen from the record, respondents 2 to 4 did not assign any 
specific reason for filing E.A.130/2022 after obtaining the sale deed. But 
they stated in their counter that due to ill-health, first respondent did not file 
execution application for delivery of possession. Similarly, RW1 testified in 
the cross-examination that after obtaining the sale deed, his father (first 
respondent) suffered with ill-health, loss of memory (alzheimer) and 
diabetes, and that they came to know about the sale deed proceedings after 
the death of their father. Thus, he explained why first respondent did not file 
E.A. immediately after taking the sale deed. However, they did not place 
any document evidencing that the first respondent suffered with ill health, 
loss of memory, diabetic, etc. Regardless of it, it is already stated above 
that E.A.122/2025 is filed within the limitation of 12 years. Therefore, an 
explanation as required U/Sec.5 of the Act is not required in the present 
case. Therefore, not assigning a reason for the non-filing of documents 
about the health condition of the first respondent does not defeat the claim 
of respondents 2 to 4. Hence, the contentions of learned counsel are not 
tenable.
75. 
Except for the above, the petitioner has not placed any other 
material to establish his right and title over schedule property or defeat the 
claim of respondents 2 to 4. On the other hand, respondents 2 to 4 
established that the fifth respondent and Narasimha Swamy obtained the 
decree collusively, and the sale deed obtained by Narasimha Swamy is also 
a collusive one. Therefore, the title would not be passed to either PW2 or 
Narasimha Swamy under their respective sale deeds.
76. 
In the light of the aforesaid facts and circumstances and the 
aforesaid discussion, it is held that judgment in O.S.947/2008 is a collusive 
one, and therefore, no title will be passed to Narasimha Swamy or PW2 or 
27
the father of petitioner under their respective sale deeds. Consequently, the 
petitioner is not entitled to declare his ownership over the schedule 
property. Accordingly, this point is answered.
Point No.2:
Whether the petitioner is entitled to a permanent injunction restraining 
the respondents from interfering with the possession and enjoyment 
of schedule property as prayed for?
77. 
The petitioner sought a permanent injunction. It is outside the 
purview of Rules 97 r/w 101 of O.21 of the Code. Therefore, the petitioner is 
not entitled to seek even a permanent injunction. However, it is already 
stated above that the petitioner is not entitled to a declaration. 
Consequently, the petitioner is not entitled to a permanent injunction. 
Accordingly, this point is answered.
78.
In the result, this petition is dismissed.
